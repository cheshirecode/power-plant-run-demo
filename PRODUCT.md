# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Power Plant Run is for casual dev friends with a strategy game mindset. They are likely playing in short shared sessions, sending a room link around, testing riskier routes, and laughing at near-misses while still wanting the mechanics to be legible enough to optimize.

## Product Purpose

The product is a browser-based cooperative arcade strategy game where players log in with GitHub, join a room, race to repair power plant nodes, manage a countdown, and survive the blast. Success means the room loop is immediately understandable, tense within seconds, and interesting enough for friends to replay while comparing final scores.

## Brand Personality

Tense, readable, arcade-cooperative. The tone should feel energetic, playful, and slightly wacky, with enough tactical clarity that players can make real decisions under pressure.

## Anti-references

Do not make the game feel like a SaaS dashboard, productivity app, polished enterprise tool, or generic dark neon tech demo. Avoid sterile panels, over-explained helper copy, bland corporate spacing, and interface chrome that competes with the canvas. Do not sand away the weirdness: the game should stay fun, expressive, and a little ridiculous.

## Design Principles

1. Pressure first: the timer, blast risk, and repair rewards should be visible before players need to read instructions.
2. Readable chaos: the board can be busy and wacky, but player positions, node values, and final scores must remain scannable.
3. Strategy in motion: every node should imply a tradeoff between distance, risk, value, and time extension.
4. Friends over accounts: GitHub identity should feel like lightweight presence, not an account-management product.
5. Celebrate the round: end states should make the score outcome unmistakable and easy to share or restart from.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target practical WCAG AA contrast for surrounding UI and keep canvas-critical information redundant where possible: node value labels, shape/position, color, and motion should work together. Do not rely only on color to communicate repaired nodes, blast danger, or player control. Keep motion snappy but avoid flashing that dominates the screen outside the countdown's intentional urgency.
